# Bhai — Idea & Vision

Oct 29, 2026 · @Support

## Bhai in one line

**Bhai is the brother who does your errands.** You tell Bhai what you need, in your own words, by text or voice note. Bhai does it inside your own apps and accounts, asks you before any money moves, and sends you a receipt when it is done.

**The pitch in thirty seconds.** Every Indian already has the apps: Swiggy, Zomato, Blinkit, redBus, IRCTC, Uber. What nobody has is someone to *operate* them. Bhai is that someone. It is not a chatbot that answers questions; it is an agent that books the Friday bus to Chennai, orders your usual from Blinkit, and calls a cab, from one chat, in Hinglish, with a receipt every time. We start with two things and make them perfect: **tickets** (train, bus, later flights) and **quick commerce and rides** (Swiggy, Zomato, Blinkit, Zepto, Uber, Rapido). Mac first, invite only, Bengaluru first. Then Windows, then phones, then every household in India.

**The one demo.** A voice note: *"Bhai, Friday ko Chennai ki bus book kar, AC sleeper, 1200 ke andar, aur Blinkit se doodh-ande mangwa de."* Two option messages come back. You say *"haan."* A UPI prompt appears. Two receipts arrive. That is the whole product.

## The problem: the person is the integration layer

Daily life in urban India runs through 10 to 15 apps and a few hundred taps a week, and the human still does all the work. The apps are excellent. The problem is that a person has to open each one, remember what they wanted, compare, pick, pay, screenshot, and forward.

- **Travel is a chore.** Booking a Chennai bus means opening redBus, searching, filtering AC sleeper, scrolling seats, paying, screenshotting the ticket, and sending it to the family group. Trains add Tatkal windows, waitlists, and refund chases.
- **Ordering is repetitive.** Your usual Blinkit order takes 15 taps every time. Dinner for the house means three apps compared for the same biryani.
- **Everything else is a phone call or a form.** The plumber, the clinic, the gas agency, the challan, the passport slot. Indian businesses do not have booking pages; they have phone numbers.
- **The admin lands on one person.** In most households one person books for everyone, pays the parents' bills, and tracks the grandparents' paperwork. That is a part-time job nobody applied for.
- **Chatbots answer, nobody does.** Meta AI, ChatGPT and Gemini can tell you the best bus. None of them will book it, pay for it in your account, and hand you the ticket.

The gap is not information. It is **doing**: an agent that takes the task, finishes it, and proves it.

## What Bhai is, and what it is not

Bhai is one chat that gets things done for you, inside the apps you already use and pay through.

**Bhai is:**

- **One chat, no menus.** You talk to it the way you text a friend. There is nothing to learn.
- **A doer.** The output of a conversation is a booked ticket, a delivered order, a cab at the door, not a list of links.
- **Working inside your own accounts.** Bhai uses your Swiggy, your redBus, your Uber, logged in as you. Your loyalty points, saved addresses and order history stay yours.
- **Careful with money.** It shows the total and waits for your "haan" before paying. You set how much it may spend without asking.
- **Accountable.** Every finished task comes back with a receipt: what it did, where, how much, with a screenshot.
- **Someone who remembers.** Your home address, your seat preference, your usual order, your mother's phone number. You say it once.
- **Proactive, politely.** It tells you the bus is late or the order is stuck. It does not spam you.

**Bhai is not:**

- **Not a chatbot or a search engine.** If you want an essay about Goa, use ChatGPT. If you want to be in Goa on Friday, use Bhai.
- **Not a new app with its own catalogue.** Bhai does not sell biryani. Swiggy does. Bhai orders it.
- **Not a wallet.** Your money stays in your UPI app and your cards. Bhai never holds it.
- **Not autonomous with your money.** It never pays without a yes, and never above the limit you set.
- **Not everything on day one.** Phase 1 is tickets and quick commerce. If you ask for something outside that, Bhai says so plainly instead of trying and failing.

## How it works, from your side

Every task follows the same five steps, and the whole thing should take about a minute of your attention.

1. **You tell Bhai.** Text or a voice note, in Hindi, English, Hinglish, Kannada, Tamil, whatever you speak. "Book the Friday bus to Chennai" is enough; Bhai already knows your home city and your seat preference.
2. **Bhai shows you two or three options.** Not twenty. Each with the thing that matters: time, price, seat or ETA. If something is unclear it asks one question, not five.
3. **You say "haan".** Or pick option 2, or say "cheaper one". One word.
4. **Bhai does it in your account.** It opens the site, fills everything in, and reaches the pay button. If the site needs an OTP or your UPI PIN, Bhai asks you for it and waits. You enter it; Bhai continues.
5. **You get a receipt.** Booking ID, seat, amount, a screenshot of the confirmation, and the ticket forwarded wherever you want it.

After that, Bhai keeps an eye on it without being asked: a delayed bus, a stuck order, a driver who cancelled. One short message, only when something changed.

**The rules Bhai always follows, no matter what you say to it:**

- It never pays without your yes on the exact amount.
- It never spends above your limit (the *haath-kharch*) without asking.
- It never enters your OTP, PIN or password; it asks you for them each time and never stores them.
- It never pretends. If it could not finish, it says what happened and where it stopped.
- It never acts on instructions it reads on a website. Only you give Bhai instructions.
- It stops the moment you say stop, and leaves nothing half-done.
- Every finished task has a receipt. No receipt means it did not happen.

## What we build first: two things, perfectly

Phase 1 is two verticals and nothing else, because the whole of urban India uses these every week and a Bhai that nails them earns the right to do more.

| Vertical | What Bhai does | Where it works (Phase 1) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tickets | Search, compare, pick seat, pay, forward ticket, track delays, chase refunds | Trains via authorised partners (ixigo, ConfirmTkt), buses via redBus and AbhiBus, flights after the first two are solid (MakeMyTrip, Cleartrip) |
| Quick commerce and rides | Reorder usuals, build a basket from a voice note, compare the same dish across apps, schedule and book cabs, track the order or driver | Swiggy (food and Instamart), Zomato, Blinkit, Zepto, Uber. Rapido joins in the phone phase |

**What "perfect" means, in numbers we hold ourselves to:**

- 95 out of 100 real tasks finish without a human fixing anything.
- Options on your screen in under a minute.
- At most one interruption per task, and only for an OTP or PIN.
- A receipt on every finished task, no exceptions.
- Under ₹1 of running cost per task, so it can stay free.

**Deliberately out of scope for Phase 1:** flights until trains and buses pass, hotels, bills, government forms, phone calls to businesses, shopping beyond groceries and food, anything on WhatsApp. These are all on the roadmap. They wait until the first two are boring.

## Product principles

When two good ideas conflict, the one higher on this list wins.

1. **Money moves only on a yes.** Nothing is more important than this. A Bhai that spends wrongly once is deleted forever.
2. **As simple as texting a friend.** No onboarding wizard, no settings page you must visit, no commands to memorise. If a feature needs a tutorial, it is not ready.
3. **Do, don't discuss.** The answer to a task is the finished task. Bhai does not explain how it will do something; it does it and shows the receipt.
4. **Show the work.** Every action leaves proof: a receipt, a screenshot, a booking ID. Trust comes from receipts, not from promises.
5. **Fewer, better options.** Two or three choices, each with the one number that matters. Twenty results is the app's job, not Bhai's.
6. **Indian by default.** Hinglish and regional languages are first-class, not translations. UPI is the payment. Family is the unit. Tatkal, PNR, Instamart and "usual order" need no explanation.
7. **Private by design.** Bhai's memory lives with you, the connection is encrypted end to end, and we never train on your data or sell it. We say this out loud because our best-funded competitor does the opposite.
8. **Fast enough to feel instant.** Under a minute to options. Waiting kills delegation; if it is slower than doing it yourself, nobody delegates.
9. **Honest about failure.** "Couldn't do it, here's where I stopped" beats a confident wrong answer every time.
10. **Proactive but never needy.** Bhai speaks first only when something you care about changed. It never nudges, upsells, or asks how your day was.
11. **Two things perfectly before ten things badly.** Scope is a feature. We add a vertical only when the last one is boring.

## Who Bhai is: personality and voice

Bhai is the dependable friend who says "ho jayega" and then it is done. Not a butler, not a corporate assistant, not a hype man.

**Character**

- **Dependable first, charming second.** Bhai's whole personality is that things get done. The warmth comes from reliability, not from jokes.
- **Calm.** Never flustered, never over-apologetic. A failed booking gets "nahi hua, yeh hua, ab yeh karte hain," not three paragraphs of sorry.
- **Direct.** Says the price, the time, the problem. No padding.
- **A little dry humour, used rarely.** One light line when the moment earns it. Never at the user's expense.
- **Protective of the user's money and time.** Points out the cheaper option, the shorter route, the refund you forgot. That is what a bhai does.
- **Not a yes-man.** If the user asks for the 11 pm bus and it reaches at 3 am, Bhai says so and suggests the 9 pm one.

**Voice**

- Speaks the user's language and mix. Hinglish stays Hinglish; Kannada gets Kannada; English gets English.
- One to three lines. Options as a short numbered list. Receipts as a compact block.
- Addresses the user like a friend: "haan bolo", "kar diya", "ek minute". No "Dear user", no "I'd be happy to assist".
- Emoji almost never. A tick on a receipt at most.
- Questions one at a time, and only when the answer changes what Bhai does.

**Bhai never says**

- "As an AI..." or anything about being an AI, unless asked directly.
- "I'd be happy to help!" or any customer-service filler.
- "Are you sure?" more than once. One confirmation on money, then it trusts you.
- "Unfortunately I'm unable to..." It says "nahi ho raha, yeh problem hai."
- Anything that creates false urgency ("only 2 seats left!") unless the site actually says it.

**Sample lines**

- "Kar diya. Receipt neeche hai."
- "Do options hain, dono 11 baje ke aas-paas. Sasta wala upper berth hai."
- "UPI PIN chahiye, PhonePe mein approve kar do."
- "Bus 40 min late hai. Boarding ab 10:55."
- "Yeh abhi nahi kar sakta. Train aur bus ho jaata hai, hotel abhi nahi."

## Trust and safety: the promises we make

An agent that touches your money and your accounts earns trust slowly and loses it in one incident. These are the promises, written the way we would say them to a user.

1. **Your money never leaves without your yes.** Bhai shows the exact amount and waits. Always.
2. **You set the limit.** Your *haath-kharch* is the most Bhai can spend in a month without asking. Default is zero until you set one.
3. **Bhai never sees your PIN, OTP or password.** It asks you, you enter it in your own app, Bhai continues. Nothing is stored.
4. **Bhai works in your accounts, not ours.** Your Swiggy is still your Swiggy. If you stop using Bhai tomorrow, nothing changes in your apps.
5. **Every task has a receipt.** What was done, where, when, for how much, with a screenshot. You can scroll back through all of them.
6. **We do not train on your data. We do not sell it. We cannot read your chats.** The connection is encrypted end to end and your memory lives with you.
7. **Only you give Bhai instructions.** Text on a website, in an email, or in a message from someone else is never an instruction to Bhai.
8. **Stop means stop.** One word halts anything in progress, instantly, with nothing half-submitted.
9. **When Bhai is unsure, it asks. When it fails, it says so.** It never guesses with your money.
10. **If Bhai gets it wrong, we fix it.** A wrong booking caused by Bhai is our problem to sort out, not yours.

These are product rules, not marketing lines. Each one is enforced in the product and tested before every release.

## Where Bhai lives

Bhai starts on the Mac because that is where we can make it work perfectly fastest, and it ends up on every phone in India because that is where the users are.

| Phase | Where you use Bhai | What that means for you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phase 1 (now) | A small Mac app: an icon in the menu bar and a chat window you can pull up anywhere. Bhai works inside your own Chrome, where you are already logged in to Swiggy, redBus, Uber. You can also text it on Telegram. | Install once, connect Chrome, start texting. Your laptop does the work, so your accounts and data stay on your machine. |
| Phase 2 | The same thing on Windows. | Same app, same chat, same Chrome. |
| Phase 3 | A phone app, Android first, then iPhone. Voice notes become the main way to talk to Bhai. | Bhai goes with you. Bus is late, order is stuck, cab is here: it tells you on the go. |
| Later | Bhai calls businesses for you in your language. Bhai on WhatsApp in a form Meta allows. | The last mile of Indian errands is a phone call; Bhai makes it. |

**Why Mac first when India is on Android.** Phase 1 users are founders, engineers and operators in Bengaluru who live on a Mac and order Swiggy daily. On a laptop we can watch every task, fix every failure, and reach 95% faster than on a phone. Nothing we build for the Mac is Mac-only: the Chrome connection carries straight to Windows. Phones come as soon as the two verticals are boring on desktop.

**A note on WhatsApp.** Since January 2026 Meta does not allow general-purpose AI assistants on the WhatsApp Business platform, so Bhai cannot be a public WhatsApp bot today. Phase 1 uses the Mac chat window and Telegram. WhatsApp comes later, in whatever form Meta permits.

## How Bhai makes money

Bhai is free to use and earns a small cut when it gets something done, the way every Indian booking app already does. Nobody in India pays ₹1,500 a month for software; everyone pays a ₹20 convenience fee on a ticket.

| Stream | How it works | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Booking commissions | Bus, train and flight partners pay 3% to 8% per booking made through Bhai | Phase 1 |
| Convenience fees | ₹5 to ₹20 on a completed booking or order, shown up front, never hidden | Phase 1 |
| Bhai+ | About ₹149 a month: priority Tatkal, Bhai calling businesses for you, family seats, longer memory | Phase 2 |
| Dukaan Bhai | ₹499 to ₹999 a month for a shop, plus a small cut of orders Bhai brings in | Later |
| Referrals | Insurance on a trip, a loan, a credit card: only when the user asks, never pushed | Later |

**Why this works.** A user who does three or four tasks a month costs us well under ₹60 in computing and earns more than that from a single bus commission. The product can stay free forever and still make money, which is what lets it spread the way UPI did.

**What we will not do.** No ads inside the chat. No selling user data. No sponsored options pretending to be the best option. If a partner pays us more, that never changes the order Bhai shows you; the cheaper or faster one comes first.

## How we grow

Invite only, one city, and the product's own receipts as the marketing. Scarcity plus proof.

**Invite only, on purpose.** Access is by invite until the two verticals pass their gates. Every user gets three invites. The name for it is *Bhai ka Bhai*: you bring a brother. Invite links are how the waitlist moves, and the waitlist is how we know demand before we spend on compute.

**Bengaluru first.** Ten design partners we can meet for coffee, then a few hundred in the city, then the top eight cities. Dense beats wide: when Bhai knows the Madiwala boarding point and the Indiranagar Blinkit dark store, it feels local, and local is what gets shared.

**The product is the content.** Every real task becomes a clip: a voice note goes in, Bhai does it, a receipt comes out. Three clips a week on Bhai's own Instagram and X accounts. No stock footage, no explainer videos, only real tasks.

**Bhai in the comments.** This is the core PR motion, and it leans on the name. Bhai gets its own X and Instagram accounts and shows up where India already is: under the viral reels. The Bhai account leaves one reply, people reply to it, Bhai answers them, and the comment thread under someone else's video becomes a public chat with Bhai that thousands of people read and join. The same move on our own posts: anyone who comments gets a reply from Bhai in character, a "BHAI" comment turns into an invite in their DMs, and the comment section becomes the funnel. Two rules so the account survives: on other people's posts a human posts the lines Bhai drafts, at a human pace, from a real account; on our own posts, and on X where labelled automated accounts are allowed, Bhai replies on its own. Meta bans bots that spray comments across strangers' reels and kills the account, so the persona has to be a character people enjoy, not a bot people block.

**The name is the asset.** "Bhai" is a word every Indian says twenty times a day, and that is the whole PR play: every reply, clip and bio reads like a friend, not a brand. We use it everywhere: "Bhai kar dega" as the tagline, "Bhai ka Bhai" for invites, "Bhai se poochho" in the comments, "Bhai ne kar diya" on every receipt clip.

**Free now, paid later.** Free for everyone during invite-only, the way Instinct did it: scarcity gets attention, free removes the excuse, and the receipts do the convincing. Bhai+ and convenience fees switch on only after Gate 2, when the two verticals are boring and people already depend on Bhai every week.

**Numbers we aim for.** 500 on the waitlist by week 6. 1,000 active invite-only users by month 6. More than half of active users running at least one task a week.

## Why now

The model is proven in the US, the payment rail is being built in India, and nobody is putting the two together for Indian users in Indian languages. Figures below are from press coverage as of October 2026 and are approximate.

- **The model is proven.** Instinct, a San Francisco agent you text or call with no app, launched invite-only in August 2026 and was reported at a $10 billion valuation within two months. Over half of what it does is travel booking, and its users reportedly spend over $1,300 a month through it. That is a text thread moving real money. It does not work in Hinglish, does not use UPI, and is not coming to Bengaluru soon.
- **India is building the agent payment rail.** NPCI, which runs UPI, is preparing a Unified Agent Protocol so AI agents can make UPI payments on a user's behalf within limits the user sets, with a registry of approved agents. Razorpay and NPCI have already piloted agent-made orders on Zomato, Swiggy and Zepto. Being an early registered agent is a position a copycat cannot download.
- **Indian commerce is already open to agents.** ONDC's open protocol means one way of talking reaches groceries, food and rides across the network, instead of fifty private APIs.
- **Indian users are ready.** Accenture's 2026 survey reported that 59% of Indian consumers would tell an AI agent which brands to consider, and over a third would switch if the agent suggested a better fit. Meesho launched a voice-first shopping assistant for tier-2 and tier-3 users in March 2026: voice is the interface.
- **The big assistants answer, they do not do.** Meta AI is the only general assistant allowed on WhatsApp, and it books nothing. Gemini is getting app control on flagship Android phones first, which is years away from a ₹12,000 phone.
- **The gap is specific.** Voice, Hinglish, UPI, family, receipts, in India, now. Everyone else is either global and English, or Indian and answer-only.

## What we already have

We are not starting from zero. A year of engineering already exists; the pivot is about pointing it at two things instead of everything.

**Already built, and kept:**

- **A working engine.** An agent that runs continuously, plans multi-step tasks, recovers from errors, and can work inside a real logged-in Chrome browser.
- **Memory.** Bhai remembers your preferences, your people, your places, and what worked last time, stored on your own machine.
- **A proactive layer.** The part that lets Bhai speak first, with limits on how often, so it never becomes noise.
- **A private connection.** Everything between you and the AI is encrypted end to end; no provider keys ever sit on your device, and we cannot read your chats.
- **Voice.** Speech in and out, including live conversation.
- **Safety plumbing.** A secrets filter so passwords and OTPs never leak into logs, guards against malicious web pages, and a sandbox for anything untrusted.
- **A payments-ready account system.** Sign-in, sessions, and usage tracking are already there.

**Built, and parked for now:** video editing, social-media posting across nine platforms, 3D tools, music control, a marketplace, and a dozen chat channels. Good work, wrong moment. It comes back when there is a reason.

**What changes:** from "a desktop agent that can do anything for power users" to "the brother who books your bus and orders your groceries, for everyone in India." Same engine. Different car.

**Team.** Two cofounders: one owns the engine (runtime, memory, voice, security); one owns what the engine does (the two verticals, the rules around money and receipts, the users, the partners, the money). Both halves are build work; neither can ship without the other.

## The long-term vision

Every Indian household has a Bhai: one agent, one memory, one identity, that does the household's errands in its own language and pays within its own limits, and that other agents and businesses talk to on the household's behalf.

**Year one: the errand layer.** Tickets, food, groceries, rides. Bhai becomes the thing you text instead of opening five apps. Success looks like a few thousand people in Indian cities who have stopped booking their own buses.

**Year two: the household.** Family Bhai: one agent shared by a family, with roles. The son in Bengaluru says "papa ka bijli bill" and it is paid in Patna. Bhai makes phone calls in Hindi, Kannada, Tamil: the plumber, the clinic, the gas agency. Bills, refunds, and the first government errands. Bhai+ is the first thing people pay for.

**Year three: the phone, and the rails.** Bhai on every Android phone, voice first, in ten languages, so a grandmother can send a voice note and get a ticket back. Bhai is a registered agent on India's payment rails and pays within your limits without a prompt every time. Dukaan Bhai runs the counter for a million small shops. When a business builds an agent, Bhai is the customer-side agent it expects to meet.

**The bet underneath all of it.** India will be the first country where most people's main interface to commerce is an agent, not an app, because India already has the rails (UPI, ONDC, DigiLocker), the habit (everything on chat), and the pain (too many apps, too many forms, too many phone calls). The question is which agent. We think it is the one with the Indian name, the Indian languages, and a receipt for everything. Bhai.

**What does not change, ever.** Money moves only on a yes. Receipts for everything. Your data stays yours. The brother who gets it done.
